The Scarcity Trap

By Brad Dotinga
September 25, 2026
4 min read

A quiet month can start to feel like a verdict on your future.

A client says no. A deal stalls. Cash gets tight. Suddenly every opportunity feels like one you cannot afford to lose. You lower your price before anyone asks. You say yes to work you know is a poor fit. You check your inbox as if the next message will decide whether your business makes it.

I have seen it in business, and I have felt it myself. Scarcity can make a capable owner act from fear instead of judgment.

The pressure may be real. Bills and payroll do not disappear because you think positively. But there is a difference between facing a constraint and letting it make every decision for you.

What Scarcity Does To Your Decisions

Scarcity narrows your view. Instead of asking, "Is this the right client?" you ask, "How do I get them to say yes?" Instead of checking the margin, you focus on the revenue. Instead of building your pipeline, you spend every hour reacting.

You take on a client who drains your team. You agree to terms you would normally question. You underprice the work, then spend months trying to make up the difference.

The business gets harder to run, which makes you more desperate for the next sale. Fear creates more of the pressure you were trying to escape.

How To Get Out Of It

Start with the numbers. What cash is coming in? What is due? What is your actual runway? Which opportunities are likely to close, and which are only hopes? You may find a serious problem that needs action. You may find the situation is less dire than it feels. Either way, facts give you something to work with.

Set your standards before the next opportunity appears. Know your minimum margin, payment terms, capacity, and the kinds of clients you work best with. Decide what would make you walk away. When money is tight, you may still have to make tradeoffs. Make them deliberately, not in a moment of panic.

Keep doing the work that creates future options. Reach out. Follow up. Check in with past clients. Improve how you deliver. Build a pipeline before you need one. When one prospect is your only prospect, it is easy to give them all the power.

Talk to someone who can challenge your thinking. Scarcity gets louder when you carry it alone. A trusted peer, coach, partner, or advisor can help you separate facts from fear and see options you might be missing.

How To Stay Out Of It

You will probably feel scarcity again. A slow season, a lost account, or an unexpected expense can bring it back fast. The goal is to recognize it before it starts running the business.

Look at cash, pipeline, margins, and capacity every week. Notice when you are about to make a decision just to feel relief. Before cutting a price or taking on a difficult client, pause and ask: if I were calm and had two other options, would I still make this choice?

Keep investing in relationships when work is busy, not only when it gets quiet. Save during strong months so a slow one does not become a crisis. And remember that saying no to the wrong work protects your ability to say yes to the right work.

You do not need to pretend the pressure is not there. You need enough clarity and discipline to keep it from deciding everything for you.
