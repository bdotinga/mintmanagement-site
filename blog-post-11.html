The Tools That Build The Business

By Brad Dotinga
October 9, 2026
3 min read

I've seen business owners spend $80,000 on a truck without blinking, then hesitate over a few thousand dollars for a website, estimating software, or help with business development.

I understand why. A truck is real. You can drive it to site, load it with tools, and see it working every day. The return on sales, systems, and people can be harder to picture. But that doesn't make it any less real.

I think this mindset is especially common among those of us who built our businesses in the field. When you've spent most of your life on site, you know exactly what good equipment does for a crew. You've felt the frustration of trying to get a job done without the right tools.

That experience is valuable. It's probably part of why you were able to build a company in the first place. But at some point, you have to step back and look at the business beyond the job site.

The Crew Can Only Build What You've Sold

Without business development, where is the next project coming from? Without a sales process, how are you choosing the right opportunities and following up on the bids you've submitted? Without a solid estimating process, how do you know the work you win will actually make money?

Sales is where it starts. If there are no sales, there's no work to schedule, no crew to keep busy, and no business to grow.

That doesn't mean every owner needs to become a full-time salesperson. It means someone needs to take responsibility for building relationships, creating opportunities, and keeping the pipeline moving. Waiting for the phone to ring is a tough way to run a company.

The Systems Behind The Work

The same goes for the systems behind the work. Project management software can help your team stay on top of schedules, changes, and communication. Estimating software can make your pricing more consistent. A capable operations person can take work off your plate and help jobs run properly. A good website and thoughtful advertising can help the right customers find you before you ever meet them.

None of those things replaces skilled people in the field. They support them.

When the office is disorganized, the crew feels it. When estimates go out late, opportunities disappear. When no one follows up, bids sit unanswered. When the owner is handling every decision, growth eventually hits a ceiling.

What's Holding You Back

I'm not saying you should throw money at every software subscription, marketing agency, or new hire that comes along. You still need to know what problem you're solving and whether the investment is working. I'm saying these parts of your business deserve the same serious consideration you give a new piece of equipment.

Before you buy the next truck or tool, ask yourself: what is holding the business back right now?

Maybe it really is equipment. But maybe the bigger issue is that you need a steady flow of better projects. Maybe your estimates take too long. Maybe your project managers are stretched thin, or your team is trying to run a growing company with processes that worked when there were only three of you.

Maybe it's time to stop spending money on the shiny objects and start investing in what brings work through the door. Build the relationships. Improve your estimating. Put a real sales process in place. The next piece of equipment might help you complete a job, but a stronger pipeline is what helps you grow a business.
