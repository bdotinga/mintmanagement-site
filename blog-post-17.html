You're Too Busy for AI. That's Exactly the Problem.

By Brad Dotinga
November 20, 2026
5 min read

Everybody's talking about AI. Every podcast, every feed, every guy at the supply house. And you're sitting there thinking — yeah, sounds great. When exactly am I supposed to learn it?

You're on the hamster wheel seven days a week. Quotes, crews, suppliers, customers, the job that went sideways on Tuesday. By the time you sit down at night, your brain is done. The last thing you're doing is opening up some AI platform to figure out how it works.

So nothing changes. The wheel keeps turning.

It's Not That You Don't Believe In It

I talk to owners every week who get it. They know AI could help. They've seen the videos. It's not disbelief. It's bandwidth. There's no gap in the week to stop, learn a new tool, and figure out where it even fits in the business.

Here's the thing nobody says out loud: the guys who are figuring this out aren't smarter than you. They just got off the wheel for a minute — or somebody got them off it.

The Money Leaking Out While You're Busy

And while you're too busy to look at it, it's costing you real money. Not someday money. This-week money.

The phone rings while you're on a roof and nobody answers. That lead calls the next guy. Gone.

A message sits in your inbox for two days because you meant to get back to it. Gone.

A quote takes four days to get out because you're slammed, and by the time it lands the homeowner already signed with someone else. Gone.

Nobody's tracking the leads that went cold. Nobody's following up the old quotes. The paperwork gets typed in three times because nothing talks to anything else. None of it feels like a big deal on any one day. Add it up over a month and it's a job — maybe two — that you already did the work to win and then lost anyway.

Simple AI automation fixes all of that. The phone gets answered. The message gets a reply in minutes, not days. The quote goes out while you're still on site. The follow-up happens whether you remembered it or not. That's not futuristic stuff. That's Tuesday.

You Don't Have to Learn Any of It

This is the part people get backwards. You don't need to become an AI guy. You need somebody who already is one — and who actually knows your business.

That's what we do at Mint. I've spent 25 years as a subcontractor. I know what a day on the tools looks like, I know where the leaks are, and I know which systems are worth your money and which ones are toys. You don't learn a platform. You don't watch tutorials. You keep running your business, and the systems do the stuff that was falling through the cracks.

There just needs to be a decision to get off the wheel long enough to let someone build it for you.
