The Agents Are Here.

By Brad Dotinga
October 30, 2026
5 min read

Not long ago, if you wanted an AI agent working for your business, you had to buy a computer just to run it on.

I'm not exaggerating. Guys were buying a spare Mac mini, clearing a corner of the office desk, and leaving it running around the clock so their "AI assistant" would stay alive. Then they had to build the thing themselves — connect the accounts, duct-tape the tools together, fix it every time something broke.

If you weren't technical, you weren't doing it. Simple as that.

That world is gone.

The new wave of agents — tools like Muse and Grok — you just sign up. No spare computer. No build. The agent lives in the cloud, connects to your email, your calendar, your files, and gets to work. What took a technical person weeks to set up a year ago is now an afternoon, most of it just deciding what you want it to do.

And I'm not talking about a chatbot.

An Agent Isn't A Chatbot

A chatbot answers questions. You type, it types back, and then you go do the actual work yourself.

An agent does the work.

It can read your inbox and pull out what matters. Draft the replies and wait for your go-ahead. Watch things for you — prices, listings, deadlines — and tap you on the shoulder when something changes. Research a question properly instead of giving you the first answer it finds. Write the first draft of almost anything: a quote follow-up, a job ad, a tough email you've been putting off.

It works while you're on site. It works at 9 p.m. when you're at the rink. It doesn't call in sick, and it doesn't forget.

I know this because I use one every day. Mine keeps an eye on my projects, watches prices and listings for me, and checks things every single morning that I used to either do myself or not do at all.

It's the closest thing I've found to hiring a sharp office manager who never sleeps — without hiring anyone.

What This Means For A Trades Business

If you run a crew, your problem was never the tools. It's everything after 5 p.m.

The estimate that should have gone out tonight. The invoice nobody followed up on. The quote request sitting in an inbox you haven't opened since Tuesday. The customer who needed a reply and got one from your competitor first.

That's the work an agent eats for breakfast.

Picture it: you finish a measure, and by the time you're back in the truck, the follow-up email is drafted and waiting for your thumbs-up. The overdue invoices get a polite nudge every week without you remembering. Your schedule, your reminders, your paperwork — handled in the background while you do the work that actually makes money.

This isn't five years away. This is available right now, to a five-person roofing crew the same as a five-hundred-person company. That's the part most guys haven't caught up to yet: the playing field just got levelled, and the early ones are going to feel it first.

The Honest Catch

Here's what nobody selling this stuff will tell you: an agent is only as good as its setup.

Connect the wrong things, give it no direction, and it's just another app you ignore by February. The power is real, but so is the learning curve — deciding what to hand over, what it should never touch, and how it should sound when it speaks for your business.

That's not a reason to wait. It's a reason to set it up properly the first time.

Because your competitors are having this same thought right now. Some of them are already set up.

Where To Start

Start with one job. The thing you hate doing, that eats your evenings, that doesn't need your hands — just your eyes on it before it goes out. Inbox triage. Quote follow-ups. Chasing invoices. One job, done well, will convince you faster than any post like this one.
