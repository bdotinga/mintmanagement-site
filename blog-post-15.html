The Estimate Sitting in Your Truck.

By Brad Dotinga
November 6, 2026
5 min read

There's an estimate sitting in your truck right now. Maybe two. You measured the job days ago, you know the number, and it still hasn't gone out.

I know, because I've been that guy. You tell yourself you'll finish it tonight. Then tonight you're tired, the kids need you, and the paperwork slides to tomorrow. Tomorrow there's another measure, another crew problem, and the estimate slides again.

Meanwhile, the homeowner is not waiting for you.

You Didn't Lose It On Price

Here's what most guys get wrong about the jobs they lose. They assume they got beat on price. Somebody cheaper, somebody hungrier.

Most of the time, that's not what happened. What happened is the homeowner got three numbers, and yours arrived last — or never arrived at all. By the time your estimate showed up, they'd already picked someone, already felt good about that someone, and your number was just a comparison point.

Speed isn't a nice-to-have in estimating. Speed is the sale. The first good number usually wins, because the first good number feels like the company that actually wants the work.

Why Estimates Pile Up

It's not laziness. It's how the day is built.

You're on site all day, doing the work that pays. Estimating happens in the margins — the truck between jobs, the kitchen table after dinner, the Sunday you promised yourself you wouldn't work. And because nobody's watching, it slips. There's no foreman standing over your paperwork asking where it is.

Then there's the perfectionism trap. You hold an estimate back because you want to double-check one price, confirm one detail, make it a little cleaner. A day passes. Then three. The estimate that was 95% done on Tuesday is still 95% done on Friday, and the job is gone.

A good estimate sent today beats a perfect estimate sent next week. Every time.

Discipline Isn't The Fix. You're The Bottleneck.

For years my rule was: same day. Measure it in the morning, send it before bed. And it works — when you actually do it.

But be honest about why it keeps slipping. The bottleneck is you. You're the best estimator in your company, so every number waits on your evenings, your Sundays, your energy. There's a ceiling on that, and you hit it every busy season.
