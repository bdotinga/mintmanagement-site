The Loneliest Job in Construction

By Brad Dotinga
August 7, 2026
6 min read

If you've owned a subcontracting business for any length of time, you know something that most people don't.

It can be incredibly lonely.

Not because you're surrounded by people. In fact, most days you're talking to employees, customers, suppliers, and general contractors from morning until night.

But despite all of that, it often feels like you're carrying the weight of the business entirely on your own.

I've spent the last 25 years in this industry, and I've noticed something about subcontractors.

We don't like showing our cards.

We Don't Like Showing Our Cards

Ask someone how business is going and the answer is almost always, "Couldn't be better."

Meanwhile, they're lying awake at night wondering how they're going to make payroll, chasing overdue invoices, dealing with employee issues, losing bids, or questioning whether they're even making the right decisions.

I know because I was that person.

For years, I convinced myself that asking for help was a sign of weakness. I thought I had to have all the answers. I believed that if I let people see the struggles behind the scenes, they would think less of me.

So I carried everything myself.

The stress.
The pressure.
The uncertainty.

Looking back, I wish I hadn't.

Business Gets Lighter When You Stop Carrying It Alone

Because one of the biggest lessons I've learned is that business gets lighter when you stop trying to carry it alone.

Over the past few years, I've had the opportunity to spend more time with other business owners who are willing to be honest. We celebrate wins together. We talk openly about failures. We share ideas that work and warn each other about the ones that don't.

And something interesting happens.

One person's mistake becomes another person's lesson.

One person's success becomes inspiration for everyone else.

Nobody has to reinvent the wheel.

That's the kind of industry I wish I had found years ago.

It's a Knife Fight Out There

Lately, I've been hearing the same thing from subcontractors across the industry.

"It's a knife fight out there."

Margins are shrinking. Everyone seems to be undercutting everyone else. The race to the bottom is happening faster than ever.

But I don't believe that's the only path forward.

What if, instead of competing on who can make the least amount of money, we learned from each other on how to build healthier businesses?

What if we shared ideas that improved operations, strengthened leadership, increased profitability, and helped us all become better business owners?

What if we built each other up instead of constantly trying to beat each other down?

What Construction Actually Needs

I don't believe construction needs another Facebook group full of opinions.

I believe it needs a real community.

A place where subcontractors can show up honestly.

A place where asking for advice isn't seen as weakness.

A place where we celebrate wins, learn from losses, solve problems together, and remind each other that none of us has to do this alone.

That's the community I wish had existed when I started.

So I'm Going to Help Build It

So instead of wishing I could go back and find it, I'm going to help build it.

Because after 25 years in this industry, I've learned one thing for certain.

No one builds alone.

Not great companies.
Not great leaders.
And certainly not great lives.

If you've ever felt like you're carrying the weight of your business by yourself, know this.

You're not the only one.

And maybe it's time we stopped pretending we are.

You don't have to
carry it alone.
